Ireland has two economies. Only one of them is real

On paper, Ireland is one of the richest countries on earth. In practice, a large slice of that wealth belongs to multinationals that book profits here but employ few people. The statisticians built a special measure to see past the illusion.

Analysis · Data: CSO National Accounts, Department of Finance · Updated 2023–24

In 2015, Ireland's GDP grew by an astonishing 26% in a single year. Nothing in the real economy had changed by anything like that much; a handful of multinationals had simply relocated intellectual property and aircraft-leasing assets onto Irish balance sheets. The economist Paul Krugman dubbed it "leprechaun economics", and the phrase stuck because it captured a real problem: for Ireland, GDP had stopped describing the domestic economy.

So the CSO built an alternative. Modified Gross National Income — GNI* — strips out the profits of redomiciled firms, the depreciation on foreign-owned intellectual property, and aircraft leasing. What remains is a far better picture of the income actually generated by, and available to, people living in Ireland.

The gap between GDP and GNI* is now close to €200 billion — bigger than most European economies.

The corporation-tax windfall

The same multinational concentration shows up in the public finances. Corporation-tax receipts have surged to around €28 billion, having roughly doubled in a few years. It is a windfall that has pushed the exchequer into surplus — but a precarious one: the Department of Finance and the Revenue Commissioners have repeatedly warned that a large share is paid by a mere handful of firms.

Corporation tax has become a windfall — and a risk
A small number of firms account for a large share of receipts.

That concentration is why economists watch GNI*, not GDP, when they judge how much Ireland can actually afford — and why so much of the surplus is being routed into sovereign savings funds rather than day-to-day spending. The headline number flatters; the real economy is smaller, and more exposed, than it looks.
